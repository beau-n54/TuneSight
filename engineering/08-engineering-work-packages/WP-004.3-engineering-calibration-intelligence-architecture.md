## Document Authority

This document defines the canonical architecture for reusable Engineering Calibration Intelligence. It records engineering meaning, qualification, relationships and domain boundaries. It does not implement runtime behaviour.

The [Engineering Constitution](../02-engineering-constitution.md) remains the constitutional authority. The [Engineering Blueprint](../04-engineering-blueprint.md), the ratified [Engineering Architecture Bible](<../05 engineering-architecture-bible/README.md>), its [Knowledge chapter](<../05 engineering-architecture-bible/02-knowledge.md>) and the [Cross-Domain Engineering Contracts](<../05 engineering-architecture-bible/00-cross-domain-engineering-contracts.md>) govern this architecture. The [Production Contract](../06-production-contract.md) governs any future production implementation.

WP-004.3 is a child of [WP-004 Knowledge Implementation](WP-004-knowledge-implementation.md). It does not redefine Knowledge, Vehicle Identity, Evidence, Correlation, Explanation, Decision, Memory, Evolution or Presentation.

## Engineering Objective

Engineering Calibration Intelligence shall represent reusable engineering understanding of calibrations rather than expose only source table names.

The architecture shall enable qualified answers to:

- What is this calibration?
- What does it control?
- Why does it exist?
- What engineering objectives does it serve?
- How may it behave when represented values increase or decrease?
- Where may its behaviour become nonlinear?
- What boundaries, dependencies and protections govern it?
- Which systems and calibrations participate in the wider strategy?
- What evidence and authority support each assertion?

TuneSight shall improve understanding of engineering systems. It shall not use this architecture to prescribe calibration values, encourage aggressive changes or replace qualified engineering judgement.

## Domain Ownership

Knowledge is the authoritative owner of reusable calibration engineering truth, including:

- canonical Calibration Knowledge Objects;
- Purpose Assertions;
- Engineering Intent Assertions;
- Engineering Objective Knowledge;
- governed vocabularies;
- directional behaviour;
- reusable dependencies and relationships;
- applicability;
- qualification;
- provenance;
- conflict;
- version and lifecycle.

Vehicle Identity owns the identity and configuration of the current system. Evidence owns current observations and events. Correlation owns relationships between current observations. Explanation owns analysis-specific reasoning and determines why a calibration is relevant to a current condition. Decision owns current inspection priority, guidance, alternatives and risk. Presentation communicates published outputs without creating calibration meaning.

Access to Calibration Knowledge does not transfer ownership.

## Canonical Calibration Knowledge Model

A Calibration Knowledge Object is an independently identifiable reusable engineering concept.

```text
Calibration Knowledge Object
    |
    +-- Calibration Identity
    +-- Canonical Name and Qualified Aliases
    +-- Purpose Assertions
    +-- Engineering Intent Assertions
    +-- Calibration Kind
    +-- Controlled and Related Subsystems
    +-- Source Representations
    +-- Applicability
    +-- Directional Behaviour Assertions
    +-- Boundary Conditions
    +-- Protective Responses
    +-- Engineering Relationships
    +-- Qualification and Conflict
    +-- Provenance
    +-- Version and Lifecycle
```

Every independently meaningful assertion retains its own qualification. Verification of identity does not verify purpose, intent, behaviour, applicability, side effects or relationships automatically.

## Calibration Identity

Calibration Identity is a stable Knowledge identity independent of XDF table names, filenames, directories, addresses and presentation labels.

Identity may preserve:

- stable canonical identifier;
- canonical name;
- qualified aliases;
- source-specific representations;
- calibration kind;
- version;
- lifecycle;
- supersession;
- conflict.

An XDF table is a possible source representation of a calibration. It is not automatically the canonical calibration itself. Similar names do not prove identical identity. Identical names do not prove identical meaning. Addresses shall not be assumed stable across ROM families or software variants.

## Purpose Assertions

Purpose explains **what** a calibration does.

A Purpose Assertion may describe:

- the quantity represented;
- the controlled or constrained function;
- the primary subsystem;
- the immediate control role;
- the relevant strategy context.

Purpose is independently qualified. It shall not be inferred solely from an XDF name, category keyword or table address.

## Engineering Intent Assertions

Engineering Intent is a first-class canonical Knowledge assertion.

Engineering Intent explains **why** a calibration exists and which engineering objective it serves within the surrounding control strategy.

Purpose and Engineering Intent are permanently distinct:

```text
Purpose
    |
    +-- What the calibration does

Engineering Intent
    |
    +-- Why the calibration exists
    +-- Which engineering objectives it serves
    +-- Which systems participate in that strategy
```

An Engineering Intent Assertion may preserve:

- assertion identity;
- calibration identity;
- qualified intent summary;
- related Engineering Objectives;
- participating subsystems;
- strategy context;
- protected interests;
- governing constraints;
- documented trade-offs;
- applicability;
- verification;
- assertion-scoped confidence;
- authority;
- provenance;
- supporting and contradictory evidence;
- unresolved reason;
- version;
- lifecycle.

General engineering plausibility does not prove OEM Engineering Intent. Where intent cannot be established authoritatively, it remains unknown, candidate or provisional according to the available evidence.

Engineering Intent shall not prescribe calibration values, establish performance targets, recommend aggressive changes, authorise removal of protections or claim that an engineering constraint is unnecessary.

## Engineering Objective Knowledge

An Engineering Objective is a reusable Knowledge Object when it requires stable identity, reuse, relationships, qualification or lifecycle.

Objectives may include torque delivery, combustion stability, emissions control, component durability, thermal management, drivability, transient response, fuel-system protection, turbocharger protection, transmission protection, diagnostic plausibility and fail-safe operation.

This vocabulary is illustrative rather than permanently closed.

One calibration may serve multiple objectives. Multiple calibrations may cooperate toward one objective. An objective relationship shall preserve direction, applicability, authority, provenance, evidence, qualification, conflict and lifecycle.

## Calibration Kind Vocabulary

Calibration Kind shall use a governed extensible vocabulary rather than a permanently closed enumeration.

Initial concepts may include:

- target;
- limiter;
- compensation;
- conversion;
- scalar;
- axis;
- control gain;
- feed-forward;
- feedback;
- diagnostic;
- protection;
- threshold;
- enablement;
- scheduling;
- plausibility;
- unknown.

Every governed vocabulary term shall preserve stable identity, canonical meaning, version, lifecycle and compatibility or supersession where applicable.

An unrecognised kind remains unknown or provisionally classified. Consumers shall not force it into a convenient category.

## Directional Behaviour Assertions

Directional behaviour describes a qualified relationship between a represented calibration change and an expected engineering response.

A Directional Behaviour Assertion may preserve:

- manipulated quantity;
- direction or response form;
- affected engineering quantity;
- affected subsystem;
- expected effect;
- operating region;
- preconditions;
- boundary conditions;
- nonlinear characteristics;
- potential protective response;
- dependencies;
- exceptions;
- applicability;
- qualification;
- provenance;
- conflict;
- lifecycle.

Directional behaviour is descriptive Knowledge. It is not an instruction to change a value.

### Increase Behaviour

Increase Behaviour describes the supported response when the represented calibration value increases.

The architecture shall distinguish direct increase, permitted increase, increased ceiling, increased sensitivity, positive compensation and other qualified meanings. It shall not assume that an increased represented value always increases physical output.

### Decrease Behaviour

Decrease Behaviour describes the supported response when the represented value decreases.

Decrease Behaviour is independently qualified. It shall not be inferred automatically as the inverse of Increase Behaviour.

### Nonlinear Behaviour

The architecture shall support:

- piecewise response;
- saturation;
- diminishing response;
- increasing or decreasing sensitivity;
- interpolation;
- hysteresis;
- discontinuity;
- axis-dependent response;
- sign changes;
- conditional response;
- unknown response shape.

Each qualified operating region shall retain the evidence and applicability supporting it.

## Boundary Conditions

Behaviour may be bounded by:

- minimum or maximum represented value;
- RPM;
- load;
- temperature;
- pressure;
- gear;
- operating mode;
- enablement state;
- control-state transition;
- related limiter;
- hardware capability;
- software or ROM applicability;
- safety or plausibility threshold.

An XDF display range or axis does not establish an authoritative engineering boundary without appropriate evidence.

## Protective Responses

Protective Response Knowledge describes a qualified relationship between calibration behaviour, applicable conditions and a potential protection.

It may preserve:

- response identity;
- activation conditions;
- affected subsystem;
- expected intervention;
- related calibrations;
- protected interest;
- applicability;
- qualification;
- provenance;
- uncertainty.

The existence of a Protective Response relationship does not prove that the response activated in a current log. Evidence establishes observed conditions; Explanation determines current relevance; Decision owns resulting guidance.

## Engineering Relationships

Calibration relationships are first-class Knowledge.

Governed relationship meanings may include:

- controls;
- targets;
- limits;
- constrains;
- compensates;
- converts;
- depends on;
- enabled by;
- disabled by;
- interacts with;
- supports;
- protects;
- triggers potential protective response;
- serves objective;
- balances objective;
- constrained by objective;
- participates in strategy;
- represented by;
- variant of;
- applies to;
- associated with event type;
- associated with failure pattern;
- inspect before;
- supersedes.

Each relationship preserves source, target, direction, meaning, applicability, authority, provenance, evidence, verification, confidence, conflict, version and lifecycle where applicable.

`inspect before` is reusable Knowledge only where it represents an authoritative dependency rule. Current investigation order remains a Decision output.

## Source Representations

A Calibration Source Reference records how a canonical calibration is represented by a qualified source.

It may include:

- source identity and type;
- source-native identifier;
- displayed name;
- address or region where qualified;
- axes and units where qualified;
- conversion metadata where qualified;
- applicability;
- provenance;
- verification;
- lifecycle.

Source metadata may remain useful when canonical identity is unknown. It shall not be promoted beyond its evidence.

## Applicability

Calibration Knowledge may be scoped by:

- platform;
- engine family;
- ECU family;
- DME variant;
- control strategy;
- ROM family;
- software version;
- Calibration ID;
- Stock Variant;
- operating mode;
- transmission;
- region or emissions specification;
- hardware configuration;
- source XDF;
- binary address or region;
- effective version range.

Applicability may be universal, strategy-scoped, platform-scoped, ROM-scoped, exact-variant-scoped, operating-mode-scoped, conflicting or unknown.

Platform neutrality means that applicability is qualified Knowledge rather than hard-coded downstream platform branching.

## Qualification

Every independently meaningful assertion shall preserve:

- assertion identity;
- value or proposition;
- verification status;
- assertion-scoped confidence;
- authority;
- provenance;
- supporting evidence;
- contradictory evidence;
- applicability;
- conflict;
- unresolved reason;
- version;
- lifecycle.

Qualification states may include Unknown, Observed, Candidate, Provisional, Verified, Founder Verified, Authoritatively Verified, Disputed, Rejected, Superseded and Deprecated.

Confidence is not verification. Availability is not authority. Repetition does not upgrade truth. Verification of one assertion does not propagate to another.

## Provenance

Authoritative Calibration Knowledge requires traceable provenance appropriate to the assertion.

Potential sources may include OEM technical documentation, Bosch control-strategy documentation, authoritative calibration documentation, verified source descriptions, validated engineering specifications, controlled Founder evidence and governed engineering discoveries.

XDF names, filenames, directories, addresses, keyword categorisation, neighbouring tables, observed behaviour, general tuning convention, user designation and presentation wording are insufficient by themselves to establish authoritative calibration meaning or OEM Engineering Intent.

## Conflict

Contradictory identities, meanings, behaviours, applicability or relationships shall remain visible until authorised resolution.

The architecture shall not resolve conflict through record order, source convenience, majority repetition or presentation preference. Consumers may withhold a conclusion but shall not flatten conflict into certainty.

## Lifecycle

Calibration Knowledge shall support:

- registration;
- correction;
- enrichment;
- evidence addition;
- applicability refinement;
- dispute;
- conflict resolution;
- versioning;
- supersession;
- deprecation;
- rejection;
- historical preservation.

A later software release may preserve Purpose while changing Engineering Intent, boundaries, behaviour or relationships. Historical meaning shall not be overwritten.

## Lookup Contracts

A future qualified lookup may receive:

- canonical calibration identity;
- qualified source reference;
- Vehicle Identity context;
- ROM and XDF context;
- requested relationship depth.

Architectural outcomes may include:

- exact verified;
- exact candidate;
- contextual match;
- multiple candidates;
- conflict;
- unknown;
- invalid;
- runtime unavailable.

Each output shall preserve candidates, provenance, verification, confidence, applicability, conflicts, limitations and unresolved reasons.

An unavailable Engineering Intent assertion shall not make otherwise valid calibration identity or Purpose unavailable.

## Cross-Domain Contracts

### Vehicle Identity

Vehicle Identity supplies qualified current-system context. Calibration Knowledge does not redefine the identity of the system under analysis.

### Evidence

Evidence may publish observed behaviour and events. It does not define reusable calibration meaning or infer Engineering Intent.

### Correlation

Correlation may relate current observations. It does not establish canonical calibration relationships.

### Explanation

Explanation may use qualified Calibration Knowledge to determine why a calibration is relevant to the current analysis.

Engineering Intent explains why a calibration exists. Explanation establishes why current behaviour occurred. Explanation shall not redefine or upgrade Knowledge qualification.

### Decision

Decision may use qualified Calibration Knowledge and Explanation to determine current inspection priority, prerequisites, alternatives, risk and guidance.

Decision shall not convert Engineering Intent into prescribed values, permission to bypass protection or automatic performance recommendations.

### Presentation

Presentation may communicate calibration identity, Purpose, Engineering Intent, system relationships, behaviour, boundaries, current relevance, guidance, qualification and provenance.

Presentation shall not reconstruct missing intent, infer behaviour, rank inspection order or manufacture engineering meaning.

### Evolution

Evolution may propose new or amended Calibration Knowledge through governed processes. Runtime discovery shall not write directly into authoritative Knowledge.

## Failure and Unknown Behaviour

- Unknown calibration identity produces no fabricated canonical explanation.
- A known source table with unknown canonical mapping remains a qualified source reference.
- Multiple candidates remain visible.
- Conflicting applicability remains conflict.
- Missing provenance cannot produce verified Knowledge.
- Known intent does not prove current diagnostic relevance.
- Known protection does not prove activation.
- Missing Explanation withholds causal relevance.
- Missing Decision withholds current inspection guidance.
- Missing Presentation data produces truthful absence rather than fallback truth.

8. General engineering plausibility does not establish OEM intent.
9. Engineering Intent does not prescribe values or performance targets.

12. Nonlinear behaviour and boundary conditions remain explicit.
13. Protective Response Knowledge does not prove activation.
14. Relationships are first-class Knowledge.
15. Applicability is qualified data, not platform-specific branching.
16. Confidence never substitutes for verification.
17. Unknown, conflict and provisional states remain visible.

## Phased Implementation Roadmap

No implementation phase is authorised by this architecture ratification.

### Phase 1 — Repository Evidence Inventory

Audit existing XDF metadata, source descriptions, ROM associations, related-table strings, subsystem classifications, event relationships, calibration guidance and provenance. Classify every source according to its actual authority.

### Phase 2 — Contract Definition

Define and validate Calibration Identity, source-reference identity, Purpose, Engineering Intent, Engineering Objective, qualified assertions, governed vocabularies, applicability, relationship semantics, conflict, unknown behaviour, version and lifecycle.

### Phase 3 — Canonical Knowledge Foundation

Implement canonical objects, assertions, relationships, vocabularies and immutable publication boundaries within Knowledge.

### Phase 4 — Controlled Evidence Admission

Introduce governed admission for authoritative documentation, verified XDF representations, Founder evidence and controlled engineering discoveries. Preserve provisional source metadata without promotion.

### Phase 5 — Qualified Runtime Lookup

Publish qualified Calibration Knowledge through an explicit production contract. Preserve exact, candidate, contextual, conflict, unknown, invalid and unavailable outcomes.

### Phase 6 — Explanation Consumption

Allow Explanation to cite qualified Purpose, Engineering Intent, behaviour, dependencies and event relationships without altering Knowledge.

### Phase 7 — Decision Consumption

Allow Decision to establish analysis-specific inspection priority, prerequisites, alternatives, risks and rationale.

### Phase 8 — Presentation Consumption

Present published Knowledge, Explanation and Decision outputs through faithful progressive disclosure without presentation-owned meaning.

### Phase 9 — Persistence Assessment

Determine whether stable runtime contracts require persistence. Any schema work requires separate authority.

### Phase 10 — Cross-Platform Validation

Validate representative N54, B58 Gen1, B58TU / Supra, S55, S58 and future supported cases, including ambiguous names, ROM-specific representations, nonlinear behaviour, asymmetric increase and decrease behaviour, boundaries, protections, provenance, conflict and unknown states.

## Founder Architecture Reconciliation

The Founder accepts the permanent separation between Definition Authority, Calibration Authority and Runtime Evidence.

### Definition Authority

A source XDF may contain technically authoritative calibration definitions. Applicability and semantic validity shall be qualified before TuneSight relies upon those definitions for a particular ROM or calibration. Filename, directory, table name, address or category alone does not establish exact authority.

### Calibration Authority

Once an applicable definition is sufficiently qualified and deterministic decoding succeeds, the decoded values constitute authoritative Evidence of the instructions encoded in that exact Calibration revision. Within the qualified definition scope, those instructions may establish calibrated targets, requests, limits, thresholds, compensations, control parameters, protection parameters and other calibrated concepts.

### Runtime Evidence

Calibration intent does not prove actual runtime behaviour. Runtime Evidence establishes what occurred in operation. Definition Authority, Calibration Authority and Runtime Evidence may be correlated through separately governed engineering processes but shall not be collapsed into one authority claim.

### Broader Calibration Programme Boundary

The broader Calibration/XDF programme requires separate Founder-authorised architecture beyond the canonical WP-004.3 Knowledge boundary. Its accepted future direction preserves four distinct Calibration Workspace states:

1. immutable Stock Calibration;
2. immutable Current Modified Calibration as source and historical Evidence;
3. immutable evidence-backed TuneSight Suggested Calibration; and
4. user-controlled Working Calibration.

The future programme shall address qualified XDF and Definition revisions, calibrated-value extraction, Stock/Modified comparison, Calibration Knowledge integration, coherence, evidence-backed recommendations, a genuine Manual Calibration Editor, immutable Calibration Revision lineage, deterministic reconstruction, validation and exportable Calibration artifacts.

TuneSight shall develop these capabilities through controlled incremental cycles of Discovery, Architecture, Founder Audit, Ratification, Implementation, Contract Testing, Physical Validation, Regression, Founder Validation and Governance. Programme architecture shall establish a safe boundary for each useful slice without requiring speculative implementation design for every distant capability.

Calibration and Native Vehicle Data may proceed as parallel near-term engineering tracks where their dependencies permit. Vehicle Interface architecture, ECU/DME identification, qualified Live Telemetry and Direct TuneSight Logging are near-term Founder priorities requiring their own architecture and authority. Native acquisition remains distinct from WP-005.1 Engineering Run Intelligence, which remains on Engineering Hold. Diagnostics follows the Vehicle Interface and native-acquisition foundation. ECU/DME programming and flashing remain intended later capabilities and shall not delay Vehicle Interface, identification, Live Telemetry, Direct Logging or Diagnostics.

No permanent Work Package identity for either broader track is established by this reconciliation.

## Closing Declaration

Engineering Calibration Intelligence shall explain what a calibration is, what it does, why it exists, how it participates in an engineering strategy and how its behaviour is qualified.

It shall deepen engineering understanding without fabricating truth, prescribing values or replacing engineering judgement.
