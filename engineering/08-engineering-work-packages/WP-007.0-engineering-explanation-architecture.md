## Document Authority

This document defines the canonical architecture for Engineering Explanation. It establishes the internal reasoning responsibilities, first-class Explanation objects, cross-domain contracts, qualification, traceability and failure behaviour required before future implementation.

The [Engineering Constitution](../02-engineering-constitution.md) remains the constitutional authority. The [Engineering Blueprint](../04-engineering-blueprint.md), the ratified [Engineering Architecture Bible](<../05 engineering-architecture-bible/README.md>), its [Explanation chapter](<../05 engineering-architecture-bible/05-explanation.md>) and the [Cross-Domain Engineering Contracts](<../05 engineering-architecture-bible/00-cross-domain-engineering-contracts.md>) govern this architecture. The [Production Contract](../06-production-contract.md) governs any future production implementation.

WP-007.0 is the governing architectural definition for [WP-007 Explanation Implementation](WP-007-explanation-implementation.md). It preserves WP-007 as the permanent Explanation implementation work package and does not overwrite, reinterpret or renumber it.

This architecture does not implement runtime behaviour.

## Engineering Objective

Engineering Explanation shall transform qualified Vehicle Identity, Knowledge, Evidence and Correlation into analysis-specific engineering understanding.

It shall answer, where authoritative support permits:

- What explanation is supported by the current analysis?
- Why is a calibration relevant to the current condition?
- Why is another calibration not relevant, contradicted or unresolved?
- Which Evidence supports the explanation?
- Which Evidence contradicts it?
- Which competing explanations remain plausible?
- Which explanations were rejected and why?
- Which assumptions remain unresolved?
- Why is Explanation Confidence limited?
- What additional Evidence would strengthen or weaken engineering understanding?

Explanation shall publish understanding. It shall not publish inspection priority, actions, recommendations or guidance.

## Permanent Domain Ownership

### Knowledge

Knowledge owns reusable engineering truth and premises, including canonical objects, relationships, calibration Purpose, Engineering Intent, behaviour, applicability, verification, provenance, conflict and lifecycle.

Knowledge does not determine why a current observation occurred.

### Evidence

Evidence owns current validated observations, measurements, conditions and events.

Explanation cites Evidence. It does not recreate, reclassify or revalidate it.

### Correlation

Correlation owns qualified analysis-specific relationships between current observations.

Correlation does not establish causation automatically.

### Explanation

Explanation owns:

- analysis-specific reasoning;
- context qualification;
- hypothesis evaluation;
- Engineering Arguments;
- comparative explanatory support;
- assumptions;
- contradictions;
- rejected explanations;
- Confidence Derivation;
- reasoning traceability;
- conclusions and explanation status.

### Decision

Decision owns:

- inspection priority;
- actions;
- recommendations;
- alternatives;
- guidance;
- risk;
- recommendation rationale.

Explanation may establish the strongest supported contributor or comparative explanatory relevance. It shall not instruct an engineer what to inspect, test or change.

### Presentation

Presentation faithfully communicates published outputs. It shall not construct missing reasoning, infer arguments, calculate confidence, reject hypotheses or produce guidance.

## Internal Engineering Reasoning Layer

Engineering Reasoning is an explicit internal architectural layer within Explanation.

It is:

- not a new Engineering Domain;
- not a replacement for Correlation;
- not an owner of reusable Knowledge;
- not a Decision mechanism;
- not a Presentation function.

The internal reasoning sequence is:

```text
Qualified Explanation Context
    |
    v
Hypothesis Evaluation
    |
    v
Engineering Argument Construction
    |
    v
Comparative Adjudication
    |
    v
Confidence Derivation
    |
    v
Explanation Synthesis
    |
    v
Engineering Explanation
```

Each responsibility is distinct and traceable. An implementation may optimise execution but shall not collapse their engineering meanings.

## Context Qualification

Context Qualification establishes whether the supplied inputs are suitable for reasoning within the current analysis scope.

It may evaluate:

- analysis identity and scope;
- qualified Vehicle Identity;
- qualified Evidence;
- qualified Correlation;
- applicable Knowledge;
- available telemetry channels;
- missing dependencies;
- input conflicts;
- contract versions;
- reasoning-method applicability.

Context Qualification does not alter upstream qualification. It records how supplied qualifications affect the current reasoning scope.

Input states shall distinguish available, partial, conflicting, unknown, unsupported, unavailable and invalid. Operational unavailability shall not be represented as engineering unknown.

## Hypothesis Evaluation

Hypothesis Evaluation assesses a candidate analysis-specific proposition independently.

It determines:

- whether applicability requirements are satisfied;
- which premises support the proposition;
- which observations contradict it;
- which assumptions are required;
- which Evidence is missing;
- whether Knowledge dependencies are satisfied;
- whether an exclusion condition applies;
- whether the hypothesis is supported, partially supported, contradicted, unresolved, not applicable, invalid or unavailable.

Evaluation order shall not establish rank or authority.

## Engineering Argument Construction

Engineering Argument Construction assembles qualified premises, Evidence, Knowledge, Correlation, assumptions and contradictions into an explicit reasoning structure.

An argument explains how the premises relate to the proposition. It is not a list of evidence and is not replaceable by a narrative string.

## Comparative Adjudication

Comparative Adjudication evaluates multiple Engineering Arguments within one analysis scope.

It determines:

- whether one explanation is most strongly supported;
- whether multiple explanations are contributing factors;
- which alternatives remain plausible;
- which alternatives are contradicted;
- which alternatives are inapplicable;
- whether available Evidence cannot distinguish the candidates;
- whether no supported explanation exists.

Comparative explanatory support is not inspection priority. Decision alone may turn explanation outputs into prioritised guidance.

## Confidence Derivation

Confidence Derivation establishes Explanation Confidence for an analysis-specific assertion from the complete qualified Engineering Argument and comparative assessment.

Confidence is derived from the argument. It is not attached decoratively, copied from an upstream confidence value or treated as verification.

## Explanation Synthesis

Explanation Synthesis publishes the qualified Engineering Explanation from the evaluated arguments, adjudication, Confidence Derivation and reasoning trace.

Synthesis may produce a human-readable rationale, but the narrative shall not replace the structured Explanation record.

## First-Class Explanation Contracts

The following are architectural contracts. They prescribe engineering meaning, not a database, API, persistence technology, TypeScript implementation, UI structure or runtime algorithm.

## ExplanationContext

ExplanationContext is the immutable qualified input boundary for one Explanation run.

It may preserve:

- analysis identity;
- analysis scope;
- qualified Vehicle Identity references;
- qualified Evidence references;
- qualified Correlation references;
- applicable Knowledge results;
- available channels;
- missing dependencies;
- conflicts;
- input contract versions;
- reasoning-method requirements;
- provenance.

Every reference retains its authoritative owner, identity, version, qualification, confidence scope, verification where applicable, provenance, applicability, conflict and unknown or unavailable reason.

## HypothesisEvaluation

HypothesisEvaluation is the analysis-specific assessment of one candidate proposition.

It may preserve:

- hypothesis identity;
- proposition;
- analysis scope;
- applicability requirements;
- required premises;
- supporting conditions;
- contradictory conditions;
- exclusion conditions;
- required assumptions;
- missing Evidence conditions;
- evaluation status;
- Engineering Argument reference;
- reasoning-method identity and version;
- provenance.

A reusable hypothesis definition is an Explanation-owned evaluation method. A current evaluation is an analysis-specific Explanation result. Reusable engineering premises remain Knowledge-owned.

## EngineeringArgument

EngineeringArgument is a first-class Explanation object.

It may preserve:

- argument identity;
- analysis and event scope;
- proposition;
- argument status;
- qualified premises;
- supporting Evidence;
- contradictory Evidence;
- applicable Knowledge;
- Correlation references;
- assumptions;
- missing Evidence;
- missing dependencies;
- applicability assessment;
- reasoning steps;
- relationships to alternatives;
- Confidence Derivation;
- conclusion;
- method version;
- provenance.

The proposition and conclusion shall be explicitly bounded to the analysis, event, pull, operating region or other qualified scope.

Argument statuses may include supported, partially supported, contradicted, competing, unresolved, insufficient evidence, not applicable, invalid and unavailable.

## CompetingExplanationSet

CompetingExplanationSet preserves candidate hypotheses evaluated within the same scope.

It may include:

- set identity;
- analysis scope;
- candidate hypotheses;
- Engineering Arguments;
- shared Evidence;
- distinguishing Evidence;
- contradictions;
- unresolved dependencies;
- assumption burdens;
- comparative assessment;
- selection status;
- provenance.

Possible outcomes include one supported explanation, multiple contributing explanations, one primary explanation with viable alternatives, unresolved competition, all candidates contradicted, or insufficient Evidence for any conclusion.

## ExplanationAdjudication

ExplanationAdjudication records why an explanation was selected and why alternatives were not selected.

It may preserve:

- adjudication identity;
- evaluated arguments;
- shared support;
- distinguishing support;
- contradictions;
- applicability;
- dependency completeness;
- assumption burden;
- comparative ambiguity;
- selection rationale;
- status;
- method version;
- provenance.

An explanation may be less supported, unresolved, missing required Evidence, not applicable, contradicted or explicitly rejected. These states shall not be collapsed.

## ExplanationAssumption

ExplanationAssumption is a first-class analysis-specific premise whose truth is not established by the supplied owner-domain outputs.

It may preserve:

- assumption identity;
- proposition;
- reason it is required;
- source or basis;
- applicability;
- validation status;
- impact if false;
- affected hypotheses;
- affected confidence;
- resolution requirement;
- provenance.

Statuses may include established, supported, provisional, unresolved, contradicted, invalidated and not testable with available inputs.

Assumptions remain distinct from established premises.

## ExplanationEvidenceAssessment

ExplanationEvidenceAssessment records how one Evidence item participates in an argument.

Evidence roles may include:

- required support;
- corroborating support;
- contextual support;
- contradiction;
- exclusion Evidence;
- boundary Evidence;
- missing required Evidence;
- neutral observation.

It may preserve:

- Evidence identity;
- owner-domain qualification;
- analysis scope;
- role;
- relationship to the hypothesis;
- whether it is required or optional;
- contribution rationale;
- conflict;
- limitations;
- provenance.

Explanation cites Evidence and preserves its meaning. It does not redefine the observation.

## CalibrationRelevanceAssessment

CalibrationRelevanceAssessment explains why qualified Calibration Knowledge is relevant to a current analysis.

It may preserve:

- Calibration Knowledge reference;
- analysis or event reference;
- relevance status;
- relevance rationale;
- applicable Purpose;
- applicable Engineering Intent;
- relevant behaviour;
- relevant dependency;
- supporting Evidence;
- contradictory Evidence;
- missing requirements;
- assumptions;
- Explanation Confidence;
- provenance.

Relevance statuses may include directly supported, indirectly supported, contextually relevant, competing relevance, contradicted, not applicable, insufficient Evidence, unresolved and unavailable.

A calibration is not relevant merely because its name resembles an event, it belongs to the same subsystem, it appears in a generic table list, or Presentation displays it.

## CalibrationNonRelevanceAssessment

CalibrationNonRelevanceAssessment explains why a calibration is not selected as relevant within the current analysis scope.

Reasons may include:

- applicability mismatch;
- operating-region mismatch;
- controlled quantity not implicated;
- required event absent;
- required dependency unsupported;
- contradictory Evidence;
- stronger supported upstream explanation;
- insufficient Knowledge;
- insufficient Evidence;
- unresolved applicability.

Non-relevance shall distinguish rejected, not applicable, unsupported, unresolved and lower relevance. Absence of support is not Evidence of irrelevance.

## RejectedExplanation

RejectedExplanation records an affirmative analysis-specific rejection.

It may preserve:

- hypothesis identity;
- rejected proposition;
- explicit rejection reason;
- contradictory or excluding Evidence;
- Knowledge applicability;
- assumptions evaluated;
- rejection confidence;
- analysis scope;
- method version;
- provenance.

An explanation shall not be rejected merely because another explanation ranked higher. Non-selection is distinct from rejection.

## DiscriminatingEvidenceRequirement

DiscriminatingEvidenceRequirement identifies Evidence that would strengthen, weaken or distinguish an Explanation hypothesis.

It may preserve:

- requirement identity;
- relevant hypothesis;
- required observation;
- expected supporting outcome;
- expected contradictory outcome;
- applicability;
- rationale;
- current availability;
- provenance.

This contract states what Evidence would alter engineering understanding. It shall not instruct the engineer to perform a test or acquire the Evidence. Such guidance belongs to Decision.

## ExplanationConfidenceDerivation

ExplanationConfidenceDerivation is a first-class reusable Explanation contract.

A scalar confidence value alone is insufficient.

It shall preserve, where applicable:

- confidence assertion identity;
- Engineering Argument identity;
- resulting confidence;
- confidence scope;
- supporting factors;
- limiting factors;
- contradictory Evidence effects;
- unresolved assumption effects;
- missing dependency effects;
- Correlation influence;
- Knowledge qualification influence;
- applicability influence;
- comparative ambiguity;
- derivation method;
- method version;
- rationale;
- provenance.

Explanation Confidence is distinct from Evidence confidence, event confidence, Correlation strength, Knowledge confidence, Knowledge verification, Vehicle Identity confidence and Decision guidance confidence.

Upstream confidence shall not be copied into Explanation Confidence. Verified Knowledge may support premise reliability but does not verify an analysis-specific conclusion. Correlation may support a relationship but does not prove causation.

No universal arithmetic formula is required. Qualitative, ordinal, bounded, rule-based or numeric derivations are permitted when explicit, governed, explainable and versioned.

## ExplanationReasoningTrace

ExplanationReasoningTrace is a first-class structured and inspectable record of the reasoning chain.

It shall preserve:

- trace identity;
- analysis identity and scope;
- input references;
- hypothesis set;
- evaluation steps;
- Engineering Arguments;
- comparative adjudication;
- Confidence Derivations;
- final Engineering Explanation;
- reasoning-method versions;
- contract versions;
- provenance.

Each material reasoning step should identify its inputs, evaluation, output assertion, scope, method reference, qualification and unresolved condition.

The trace exposes engineering reasoning. It does not expose private source-code execution mechanics.

Historical explanations shall not be silently rewritten when reasoning methods improve.

## EngineeringExplanation

EngineeringExplanation is the qualified published output of Explanation.

It may preserve:

- explanation identity;
- analysis and event scope;
- reasoning status;
- selected proposition;
- primary Engineering Argument;
- contributing arguments;
- alternative arguments;
- Rejected Explanations;
- Calibration Relevance Assessments;
- supporting Evidence;
- contradictory Evidence;
- unresolved assumptions;
- missing dependencies;
- Discriminating Evidence Requirements;
- Explanation Confidence Derivation;
- Explanation Reasoning Trace;
- reasoning-method and contract versions;
- human-readable rationale;
- provenance.

Reasoning statuses may include supported, partially supported, contradicted, competing, unresolved, insufficient Evidence, invalid and unavailable.

The output shall not collapse these states into a Boolean diagnosis.

## Reasoning-Method Identity, Version and Lifecycle

A reusable Explanation reasoning method is an Explanation-owned method for evaluating qualified inputs. It is not canonical Knowledge and does not own the engineering premises it consumes.

A method shall preserve, where applicable:

- stable identity;
- version;
- applicable hypothesis class;
- required inputs;
- evaluation semantics;
- permitted confidence representation;
- known limitations;
- validation status;
- lifecycle;
- supersession;
- provenance.

Lifecycle may include candidate, provisional, validated, active, disputed, superseded, deprecated and rejected.

Method improvements shall preserve historical traceability. Runtime repetition or successful workflow execution does not promote a method or analysis conclusion into Knowledge.

## Reasoning Traceability

The complete reasoning chain is:

```text
Qualified Inputs
    |
    +-- Vehicle Identity
    +-- Knowledge
    +-- Evidence
    +-- Correlation
            |
            v
Explanation Context
            |
            v
Hypothesis Set
            |
            v
Hypothesis Evaluations
            |
            v
Engineering Arguments
            |
            v
Comparative Adjudication
            |
            v
Confidence Derivations
            |
            v
Engineering Explanation
```

Every material conclusion shall remain traceable to qualified inputs and the reasoning method that produced it.

## Engineering Explanation Transparency

### Why an Explanation Was Selected

Explanation shall expose:

- the selected proposition;
- strongest supporting premises;
- applicability;
- distinguishing Evidence;
- comparative support over alternatives;
- remaining limitations.

### Why Alternatives Were Not Selected

Each material alternative shall identify whether it was contradicted, not applicable, insufficiently supported, unresolved, still viable but weaker, or explicitly rejected.

### Why Confidence Is Limited

Explanation shall expose relevant contradictory Evidence, unresolved assumptions, missing Evidence, unavailable Knowledge, uncertain applicability, competing explanations, Correlation limitations and method limitations.

### What Remains Unresolved

Assumptions, missing dependencies and unresolved alternatives shall remain visible and distinct from established premises.

### What Would Strengthen or Weaken Understanding

Explanation may publish Discriminating Evidence Requirements. It shall not convert them into an instruction to inspect, test, change or accept risk.

## Explanation-to-Decision Contract

Explanation may publish:

- strongest supported contributor;
- contributing explanations;
- competing explanations;
- rejected explanations;
- comparative explanatory relevance;
- causal or dependency ordering where supported;
- unresolved assumptions;
- confidence limitations;
- Evidence that would strengthen or weaken understanding.

Explanation shall not publish:

- inspect this first;
- perform this test;
- change this calibration;
- enter a calibration value;
- pursue a performance target;
- bypass a protection;
- accept an engineering risk.

Decision consumes qualified Explanation and alone owns action priority, recommendations, alternatives, risk and guidance.

## Explanation-to-Presentation Contract

Presentation may communicate the selected Explanation, Engineering Arguments, Evidence, contradictions, alternatives, rejection reasons, assumptions, Confidence Derivation, Reasoning Trace and Discriminating Evidence Requirements.

Presentation shall not:

- create an argument;
- infer a missing premise;
- calculate Explanation Confidence;
- reject an alternative;
- promote an unresolved assumption;
- turn an Evidence requirement into guidance;
- reconstruct a reasoning path from labels or owner-domain internals.

Progressive disclosure may reduce cognitive load but shall preserve the traceability required to understand the conclusion.

## Failure and Unknown Behaviour

- Missing required Evidence produces insufficient Evidence.
- Missing Knowledge produces an unresolved Knowledge dependency.
- Conflicting Knowledge remains conflict.
- Inapplicable Knowledge produces not applicable with reason.
- Multiple supported hypotheses remain competing or contributing.
- Absence of rejection Evidence leaves an alternative unresolved rather than rejected.
- Unresolved assumptions constrain the conclusion.
- Explanation runtime unavailability remains operationally unavailable rather than engineering unknown.
- Invalid inputs produce invalid rather than a fallback conclusion.
- No reliable conclusion produces no Explanation.
- Missing Decision output produces no inspection priority.

Unknown, unresolved, unsupported, conflict, invalid and unavailable are distinct.

2. Engineering Argument is a first-class Explanation object.
3. Every material conclusion is supported by a traceable argument.
4. Hypothesis Evaluation precedes Explanation selection.
5. Confidence is derived from the argument, not attached decoratively.
6. Confidence Derivation remains inspectable and versioned.
7. Upstream confidence is not copied into Explanation Confidence.
8. Knowledge verification does not verify an analysis-specific conclusion.
9. Correlation does not establish causation automatically.
10. Missing Evidence is distinct from contradictory Evidence.
11. Non-selection is distinct from rejection.
12. Competing explanations remain visible.
13. Assumptions remain distinct from established premises.
14. Explanation publishes understanding, not guidance.
15. Decision owns inspection priority and action.
16. Presentation never reconstructs missing reasoning.
17. Historical explanations preserve input, contract and method versions.
18. Runtime repetition does not promote analysis conclusions into Knowledge.
19. Unknown, unresolved, unsupported, conflict, invalid and unavailable remain distinct.
20. Reusable engineering premises remain Knowledge-owned.
21. Reusable reasoning methods remain Explanation-owned.
22. Evidence is cited and not recreated.
23. Context Qualification does not requalify upstream truth.
24. Comparative explanatory support is not Decision priority.
25. A Discriminating Evidence Requirement is not an instruction.
26. A human-readable narrative does not replace the structured Explanation.

## Phased Implementation Roadmap

No implementation phase is authorised by this architecture ratification.

### Phase 1 — Existing Explanation Contract Audit

Inventory current reasoning, root-cause, candidate, evidence, confidence, rejection, calibration-reference, recommendation and presentation models. Identify domain overlap without changing behaviour.

### Phase 2 — Contract Definition

Define the first-class Explanation contracts, qualification states, identities, references, method versioning, failure behaviour and compatibility rules.

### Phase 3 — Context Qualification

Implement the qualified ExplanationContext boundary without altering upstream owner outputs.

### Phase 4 — Hypothesis and Argument Foundation

Implement HypothesisEvaluation, EngineeringArgument, assumptions, Evidence assessments and competing explanation sets.

### Phase 5 — Comparative Adjudication

Implement explicit selection, contribution, competition, contradiction, non-applicability and rejection semantics.

### Phase 6 — Confidence Derivation

Implement governed, inspectable and versioned Explanation Confidence derivation.

### Phase 7 — Reasoning Trace

Implement structured reasoning traceability with input, method and contract versions.

### Phase 8 — Engineering Explanation Publication

Publish qualified EngineeringExplanation outputs without guidance.

### Phase 9 — Decision Boundary Integration

Expose qualified Explanation outputs for future Decision consumption without implementing Decision responsibility inside Explanation.

### Phase 10 — Presentation Boundary Integration

Expose structured Explanation outputs for faithful future Presentation consumption without presentation-owned reasoning.

### Phase 11 — Persistence Assessment

Determine whether historical traceability requires persistence. Any schema or persistence work requires separate authority.

### Phase 12 — Founder Validation

Validate supported, competing, rejected, unresolved, insufficient, invalid and unavailable cases against representative datasets and historical traceability requirements.

## Closing Declaration

Engineering Explanation shall preserve the complete reasoning path from qualified inputs through hypotheses, Engineering Arguments, comparative adjudication and Confidence Derivation to a transparent analysis-specific conclusion.

Explanation shall develop engineering understanding.

It shall not provide engineering guidance.
