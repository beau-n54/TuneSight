# TS-STD-005

## Engineering Semantic Integrity Standard

## 1. Purpose

This Standard defines the permanent, implementation-independent obligations governing preservation of engineering meaning throughout TuneSight.

It ensures that engineering concepts, semantic identities, terminology, propositions, relationships, classifications and representations remain explicit, bounded, distinguishable, traceable and historically reproducible. It governs semantic integrity without defining semantic content or establishing Engineering Truth.

## 2. Scope

This Standard applies to every governed artefact, process and consumer that defines, references, transforms, maps, serializes, persists, publishes, retrieves, explains, decides upon or presents engineering meaning.

It is implementation-independent, architecture-independent, manufacturer-neutral, platform-neutral, storage-neutral, schema-neutral and historically reproducible.

## 3. Authority and Framework Conformance

TS-STD-005 derives authority from the Founder Vision, Engineering Manifesto, Engineering Constitution, Engineering Principles and TS-STD-000.

Ratification establishes normative governance authority only. It does not establish the correctness of any meaning, conformance of any subject, implementation completion, Knowledge admission, publication authority or production readiness.

## 5. Permanent Engineering Semantic Model

```text
Engineering Reality
    ↓
Engineering Concept
    ↓
Ontology Classification
    ↓
Canonical Semantic Identity
    ↓
Governed Vocabulary Expression
    ↓
Knowledge Entity
    ↓
Engineering Proposition
    ↓
Engineering Assertion
    ↓
Governed Relationships
    ↓
Qualified Representations
    ↓
Consumers and Historical Interpretation
```

No stage may substitute for another. Engineering reality exists independently of TuneSight. Ontology classifies kinds of concepts. Vocabulary expresses meanings through governed terminology. Knowledge Entities instantiate concepts. Propositions express claims. Assertions govern exact proposition revisions. Relationships connect exact subjects. Representations encode meaning. Consumers interpret representations only within governed scope.

## 6. Engineering Semantic Integrity Supremacy

Engineering Semantic Integrity shall always remain subordinate to engineering reality.

Engineering meaning exists to faithfully represent engineering concepts, relationships and governed understanding.

Semantic meaning shall never be preserved merely to protect historical terminology, convention, implementation, presentation, translation or existing Engineering Knowledge where governed engineering reality demonstrates that the underlying engineering meaning has materially changed.

Where engineering reality demonstrates that semantic meaning no longer faithfully represents the engineering concepts it describes, semantic representations shall evolve only through governed processes.

Historical semantic meaning shall remain historically reproducible.

Current semantic meaning shall faithfully represent the best governed engineering understanding available within its declared scope.

## 7. Engineering Meaning Integrity Principle

Engineering meaning shall always remain subordinate to engineering reality.

Engineering meaning exists to faithfully preserve the governed understanding of engineering concepts, relationships and propositions.

Engineering meaning shall never be simplified, generalised, specialised or otherwise altered solely for consumer convenience.

Where different consumers require different representations, each representation shall faithfully preserve the same governed engineering meaning or explicitly declare every material semantic transformation, simplification, loss or contextual limitation.

Presentation, serialization, persistence, transport, Explanation, reporting, translation and artificial intelligence assistance shall preserve engineering meaning rather than redefine it.

Historical engineering meaning shall remain historically reproducible.

Current engineering meaning shall faithfully represent the best governed engineering understanding available within its declared scope.

**Engineering Semantic Principle:** Engineering Semantic Integrity does not protect terminology. Engineering Semantic Integrity protects the faithful governed preservation of engineering meaning. Where engineering reality evolves, Engineering meaning shall evolve. Engineering reality shall always remain the higher authority.

## 8. Definitions

- **Engineering Concept:** A distinguishable governed understanding of an aspect of engineering reality.
- **Ontology Classification:** The governed classification identifying what kind of engineering concept a subject instantiates.
- **Semantic Meaning:** The governed engineering interpretation of a concept, proposition, relationship, classification or representation within declared scope and context.
- **Canonical Semantic Identity:** The exact governed identity by which one independently meaningful engineering interpretation remains distinguishable from other meanings. Universal identity obligations remain governed by TS-STD-004.
- **Vocabulary Expression:** A governed term or expression used to describe meaning; it is not the meaning itself.
- **Representation:** A textual, symbolic, numerical, structural, binary, graphical or other encoding through which governed meaning is expressed.
- **Interpretation:** The bounded resolution of a representation to governed meaning within declared context.
- **Semantic Context:** Conditions necessary for faithful interpretation, including scope, applicability, domain, manufacturer, platform, subsystem, operating condition, temporal boundary, units, reference frame, preconditions, exclusions and limitations.
- **Semantic Relationship:** A governed meaning connecting exact semantic subjects.
- **Semantic Equivalence:** A governed determination that two meanings are interchangeable within exact declared scope without material semantic loss.
- **Synonymy:** A governed relationship in which distinct terms express the same or sufficiently equivalent meaning within declared context; synonymy is not identity.
- **Homonymy:** A condition in which one term or representation refers to materially different meanings.
- **Semantic Ambiguity:** A condition in which available governed context cannot resolve one exact meaning.
- **Semantic Compatibility:** The governed degree to which meanings, representations or revisions may be consumed together without material reinterpretation or loss.
- **Semantic Transformation:** A governed operation that maps, translates, converts, aggregates, simplifies or otherwise changes a representation of engineering meaning.

## 9. Permanent Distinctions

Identity is not meaning. Meaning is not representation. Representation is not label. Label equality does not prove semantic equality. Label difference does not prove semantic difference. Synonymy is not identity. Similarity is not equivalence. Equivalence is not universal interchangeability. Relationship is not causation. Classification is not Qualification. Terminology is not Authority. Source-native terminology is not canonical meaning. Translation is not semantic equivalence by default. Broad meaning is not interchangeable with narrow meaning. Association is not dependency. Compatibility is not identity. Ambiguity is not conflict. Unknown meaning is not ambiguous meaning. Current semantics are not historical semantics. Serialization success does not establish semantic correctness. Consumer convenience does not permit semantic simplification. Presentation wording does not redefine engineering meaning.

## 10. Semantic Change Classification

A representation-only change modifies encoding, formatting, non-semantic ordering, faithful display wording or serialization without changing governed meaning.

A Vocabulary revision is required when term definition, preferred usage, applicability, synonymy, homonymy, mapping or lifecycle materially changes while the governed concept remains identifiable.

An Ontology revision is required when the recognised kind, class boundary or permitted structural relationship of an engineering concept materially changes. Terminology change alone does not require Ontology revision.

A Knowledge Entity revision is required when the governed representation, classification, applicability or relationships of the same enduring entity materially change.

An Engineering Assertion revision is required when subject, predicate, object, value, scope, applicability, preconditions, exclusions, limitations, unresolved dimensions or canonical proposition meaning materially changes.

A new stable identity is required when a change creates a materially different enduring referent or proposition, including a material semantic split, merge, category change or incompatible replacement.

## 11. Requirement Record Convention

Each requirement below has revision `1`, lifecycle `Active` and compatibility classification `Foundational`. Applicability, normative force, traceability, expected conformance evidence and assessable conformance criteria are stated independently for each requirement.

## 12. Normative Requirements

### TS-STD-005-REQ-001 — Engineering Semantic Integrity Supremacy

**Applicability:** Every governed engineering meaning. **Normative force:** SHALL / SHALL NOT. **Traceability:** Section 6; TS-STD-001. **Expected evidence:** Semantic revision, correction and supersession records.

Engineering meaning SHALL remain subordinate to engineering reality and SHALL NOT be preserved merely to protect terminology, convention, implementation, Presentation or existing Knowledge.

**Conformance criteria:** Current meaning reflects the best governed understanding while prior meaning remains historically reproducible.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-002 — Permanent Semantic Model Separation

**Applicability:** Every subject represented within the permanent Semantic model. **Normative force:** SHALL / SHALL NOT. **Traceability:** Section 5. **Expected evidence:** Ontology, Vocabulary, Knowledge, Assertion and consumer contracts.

Each stage of the permanent Semantic model SHALL remain distinguishable and SHALL NOT substitute for another.

**Conformance criteria:** The exact role and owner of every semantic artefact are identifiable.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-003 — Explicit Governed Meaning

**Applicability:** Every independently meaningful governed subject. **Normative force:** SHALL. **Traceability:** Semantic model. **Expected evidence:** Definitions, scope declarations and exact references.

Every independently meaningful subject SHALL preserve sufficient explicit meaning for faithful interpretation within declared scope.

**Conformance criteria:** Interpretation does not depend upon undocumented convention or consumer inference.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-004 — Canonical Semantic Identity

**Applicability:** Every independently addressable governed meaning. **Normative force:** SHALL. **Traceability:** TS-STD-004. **Expected evidence:** Semantic identity namespaces and manifests.

Every independently addressable meaning SHALL possess canonical semantic identity sufficient to distinguish it from materially different meanings.

**Conformance criteria:** One semantic identity resolves to one governed meaning within its namespace and revision context.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-005 — Semantic Identity and Referent Identity Separation

**Applicability:** Knowledge Entities, Assertions, terms, relationships and representations. **Normative force:** SHALL. **Traceability:** TS-STD-004. **Expected evidence:** Identity models and resolution tests.

Semantic identity SHALL remain distinguishable from entity, assertion, term, relationship, representation, source, publication, Registry and storage identity.

**Conformance criteria:** Equality in one identity dimension cannot establish equality in another.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-006 — Meaning and Representation Separation

**Applicability:** Every representation of governed meaning. **Normative force:** SHALL / SHALL NOT. **Traceability:** Permanent distinctions. **Expected evidence:** Multiple-representation fixtures.

Meaning SHALL remain distinct from its representations. Representation equality SHALL NOT independently prove semantic equality.

**Conformance criteria:** Multiple faithful representations can resolve to one meaning without becoming identical artefacts.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-007 — Label Non-Authority

**Applicability:** Every label, title, filename, identifier display and UI expression. **Normative force:** SHALL NOT. **Traceability:** TS-STD-002; TS-STD-003. **Expected evidence:** Negative label-matching tests.

A label SHALL NOT independently establish identity, meaning, equivalence, classification, relationship, Authority or Engineering Truth.

**Conformance criteria:** Meaning resolves through governed semantic references rather than textual resemblance alone.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-008 — Exact Semantic Context

**Applicability:** Every context-dependent meaning. **Normative force:** SHALL. **Traceability:** TS-STD-001; TS-STD-003. **Expected evidence:** Context, scope, condition and exclusion manifests.

Context-dependent meaning SHALL preserve every material condition required for faithful interpretation.

**Conformance criteria:** Material manufacturer, platform, domain, subsystem, operating, temporal, unit and applicability boundaries remain resolvable.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-009 — Semantic Scope Non-Inflation

**Applicability:** Every semantic producer, transformer and consumer. **Normative force:** SHALL NOT. **Traceability:** TS-STD-001; TS-STD-003. **Expected evidence:** Scope comparison audits.

Meaning SHALL NOT be generalised beyond governed scope, applicability, conditions or exclusions.

**Conformance criteria:** No narrower meaning is silently promoted to broader or universal meaning.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-010 — Unknown Semantic Meaning

**Applicability:** Every subject whose meaning cannot be established authoritatively. **Normative force:** SHALL. **Traceability:** TS-STD-001. **Expected evidence:** Unknown-state contracts.

Unknown semantic meaning SHALL remain explicit and SHALL NOT be replaced with a default, probable or convenient meaning.

**Conformance criteria:** Unknown remains distinct from ambiguous, conflicting, invalid and unavailable.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-011 — Semantic Ambiguity Preservation

**Applicability:** Every representation resolving to more than one plausible meaning. **Normative force:** SHALL / SHALL NOT. **Traceability:** Permanent distinctions. **Expected evidence:** Ambiguity records and resolution tests.

Semantic ambiguity SHALL remain visible until governed context resolves one exact meaning and SHALL NOT be coerced into a canonical interpretation.

**Conformance criteria:** Plausible meanings and missing disambiguating context remain preserved.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-012 — Semantic Conflict Preservation

**Applicability:** Every materially contradictory semantic interpretation or mapping. **Normative force:** SHALL / SHALL NOT. **Traceability:** TS-STD-001. **Expected evidence:** Conflict records and exact references.

Semantic conflict SHALL remain explicit and SHALL NOT be hidden through ordering, majority usage, source convenience or Presentation preference.

**Conformance criteria:** Conflicting meanings and exact scopes remain independently resolvable.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-013 — Governed Synonymy

**Applicability:** Every declared synonym relationship. **Normative force:** SHALL / SHALL NOT. **Traceability:** Vocabulary interaction. **Expected evidence:** Exact term and relationship revisions.

Synonymy SHALL be a governed relationship between exact term revisions and SHALL NOT be treated as identity.

**Conformance criteria:** The relationship declares whether equivalence is exact, contextual or approximate.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-014 — Governed Homonymy

**Applicability:** Every term associated with materially different meanings. **Normative force:** SHALL. **Traceability:** Vocabulary interaction. **Expected evidence:** Homonym records and contextual tests.

Homonymous meanings SHALL possess distinct semantic identities and explicit contextual resolution boundaries.

**Conformance criteria:** A shared label cannot collapse materially different concepts.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-015 — Equivalence Qualification

**Applicability:** Every claimed semantic-equivalence relationship. **Normative force:** SHALL. **Traceability:** TS-STD-003. **Expected evidence:** Equivalence scope, method and qualification.

Semantic equivalence SHALL declare exact scope, applicability, governing basis, limitations and lifecycle.

**Conformance criteria:** Equivalence outside the declared boundary is neither asserted nor inferred.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-016 — Similarity Is Not Equivalence

**Applicability:** Every similarity relationship or comparison. **Normative force:** SHALL NOT. **Traceability:** Permanent distinctions. **Expected evidence:** Negative equivalence tests.

Similarity, proximity, shared attributes, shared naming or statistical resemblance SHALL NOT independently establish semantic equivalence.

**Conformance criteria:** Similar and equivalent relationships remain independently represented.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-017 — Broader and Narrower Meaning Integrity

**Applicability:** Every hierarchical semantic relationship. **Normative force:** SHALL / SHALL NOT. **Traceability:** Ontology and Vocabulary interaction. **Expected evidence:** Directional relationship records.

Broader and narrower meanings SHALL preserve direction and semantic difference and SHALL NOT be treated as interchangeable where specificity would be lost.

**Conformance criteria:** Every traversal declares lost, inherited or additional semantic constraints.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-018 — Relationship-Kind Integrity

**Applicability:** Every governed semantic relationship. **Normative force:** SHALL. **Traceability:** Knowledge relationship architecture. **Expected evidence:** Relationship-kind identities and validation.

Every semantic relationship SHALL preserve exact source, target, direction, kind, scope, applicability, qualification and lifecycle where applicable.

**Conformance criteria:** One relationship kind cannot silently become or imply another.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-019 — Association Is Not Causation

**Applicability:** Associations, correlations, sequences and causal relationships. **Normative force:** SHALL NOT. **Traceability:** Correlation and Explanation boundaries. **Expected evidence:** Negative causation tests.

Association, correlation, sequence, proximity or repeated co-occurrence SHALL NOT independently establish causation.

**Conformance criteria:** Causal meaning requires its own exact governed relationship and qualification.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-020 — Classification and Qualification Separation

**Applicability:** Every classified and qualified subject. **Normative force:** SHALL. **Traceability:** TS-STD-003. **Expected evidence:** Classification and Qualification contracts.

Classification SHALL remain distinct from Qualification and SHALL NOT independently establish Authority, Verification, Confidence or Engineering Truth.

**Conformance criteria:** Classification and Qualification remain independently inspectable.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-021 — Governed Manufacturer Terminology Mapping

**Applicability:** Every manufacturer, supplier, platform or source-native terminology mapping. **Normative force:** SHALL. **Traceability:** Vocabulary interaction. **Expected evidence:** Exact source and canonical references.

Every mapping SHALL preserve source identity, source term revision, canonical semantic target, applicability, equivalence classification, provenance, qualification and lifecycle.

**Conformance criteria:** No source-native term becomes universal or canonical merely through prevalence.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-022 — Partial and Absent Mapping

**Applicability:** Every non-exact or unavailable terminology mapping. **Normative force:** SHALL. **Traceability:** Unknown and ambiguity obligations. **Expected evidence:** Partial and absent-mapping fixtures.

Partial equivalence, unresolved mapping and absence of a canonical equivalent SHALL remain explicitly representable.

**Conformance criteria:** Mapping incompleteness cannot be concealed through approximate substitution.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-023 — Translation Integrity

**Applicability:** Every translation between languages or terminology systems. **Normative force:** SHALL / SHALL NOT. **Traceability:** Semantic transformation. **Expected evidence:** Source and target references and loss declarations.

Translation SHALL preserve source meaning, intended target meaning, context, qualification and material loss and SHALL NOT imply semantic equivalence by default.

**Conformance criteria:** Every translated representation remains traceable to its source and equivalence boundary.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-024 — Semantic Transformation Declaration

**Applicability:** Every semantic transformation. **Normative force:** SHALL. **Traceability:** TS-STD-002. **Expected evidence:** Transformation identity, inputs, outputs and rules.

Every transformation affecting interpretation SHALL declare its operation, exact inputs, outputs, method, assumptions and limitations.

**Conformance criteria:** Meaning preservation, narrowing, broadening and loss are determinable.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-025 — Information-Loss Preservation

**Applicability:** Every lossy semantic transformation or representation. **Normative force:** SHALL / SHALL NOT. **Traceability:** Transformation declaration. **Expected evidence:** Loss manifests.

Material semantic loss SHALL remain explicit and SHALL NOT be hidden by successful conversion, serialization, storage or rendering.

**Conformance criteria:** Consumers can identify every material distinction no longer preserved.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-026 — Serialization Semantic Fidelity

**Applicability:** Every serialization of governed meaning. **Normative force:** SHALL / SHALL NOT. **Traceability:** Representation separation. **Expected evidence:** Round-trip and canonical-vector tests.

Serialization SHALL preserve governed meaning and SHALL NOT create semantic equivalence merely because artefacts serialize identically.

**Conformance criteria:** Round trips preserve exact meaning and context or declare material loss.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-027 — Persistence and API Semantic Fidelity

**Applicability:** Persistence, APIs, queues, adapters and external interfaces. **Normative force:** SHALL / SHALL NOT. **Traceability:** Cross-domain contracts. **Expected evidence:** Storage and interface audits.

Infrastructure SHALL preserve semantic identity, revision, context, qualification, ambiguity, conflict and lifecycle and SHALL NOT reinterpret engineering meaning.

**Conformance criteria:** Retrieval and transport return semantically faithful governed material.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-028 — Representation-Only Change Boundary

**Applicability:** Every proposed representation-only revision. **Normative force:** SHALL. **Traceability:** Section 10. **Expected evidence:** Before-and-after semantic comparison.

A change SHALL be representation-only only where governed engineering meaning remains materially unchanged.

**Conformance criteria:** No concept, proposition, relationship, scope, applicability or interpretation changes.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-029 — Vocabulary Change Boundary

**Applicability:** Every governed term revision. **Normative force:** SHALL. **Traceability:** Section 10. **Expected evidence:** Term revision and compatibility records.

Material change to term definition, applicability, mapping, synonymy, homonymy or lifecycle SHALL create a governed Vocabulary revision.

**Conformance criteria:** Historical term meaning remains reproducible and materially different concepts do not reuse identity.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-030 — Ontology Change Boundary

**Applicability:** Every proposed Ontology change. **Normative force:** SHALL. **Traceability:** Section 10. **Expected evidence:** Ontology compatibility analysis.

A material change to recognised concept kind, class boundary or permitted structural relationship SHALL be an Ontology revision rather than terminology change.

**Conformance criteria:** Ontology and Vocabulary evolution remain distinguishable.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-031 — Knowledge Entity Change Boundary

**Applicability:** Every semantically evolving Knowledge Entity. **Normative force:** SHALL. **Traceability:** TS-STD-004. **Expected evidence:** Entity revision and semantic-change manifests.

Material change to the governed meaning of the same enduring Knowledge Entity SHALL create an exact entity revision.

**Conformance criteria:** Historical and current meanings remain independently addressable.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-032 — Engineering Assertion Change Boundary

**Applicability:** Every evolving Engineering Assertion. **Normative force:** SHALL. **Traceability:** TS-STD-004. **Expected evidence:** Assertion revision and proposition comparison.

Material change to an Assertion's governed proposition SHALL create a new exact Assertion revision.

**Conformance criteria:** No altered proposition remains hidden beneath an unchanged revision.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-033 — New Stable Identity Boundary

**Applicability:** Every semantic split, merge, category change or incompatible replacement. **Normative force:** SHALL. **Traceability:** TS-STD-004. **Expected evidence:** Identity-change and migration assessments.

A materially different enduring referent or proposition SHALL receive a new stable identity where retaining the former identity would create semantic ambiguity.

**Conformance criteria:** Stable identity represents genuine continuity rather than administrative convenience.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-034 — Semantic Compatibility Classification

**Applicability:** Every semantic revision, mapping, transformation or interchange. **Normative force:** SHALL. **Traceability:** TS-STD-000. **Expected evidence:** Compatibility assessment and rationale.

Semantic compatibility SHALL be explicitly classified as exact, representation-compatible, compatible extension, contextually compatible, partial, unknown, conflicting, incompatible, breaking or superseded as applicable.

**Conformance criteria:** Structural compatibility cannot substitute for semantic compatibility.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-035 — Directional Semantic Compatibility

**Applicability:** Every conversion or mapping where information differs by direction. **Normative force:** SHALL. **Traceability:** Compatibility classification. **Expected evidence:** Forward and reverse assessments.

Semantic compatibility SHALL be assessed directionally where conversion in one direction loses or adds material meaning.

**Conformance criteria:** Forward compatibility does not automatically imply reverse compatibility.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-036 — Semantic Correction and Supersession

**Applicability:** Incorrect, obsolete, replaced or withdrawn semantic material. **Normative force:** SHALL / SHALL NOT. **Traceability:** Semantic Integrity Supremacy; TS-STD-004. **Expected evidence:** Correction and supersession records.

Semantic meaning SHALL be corrected, deprecated, superseded or withdrawn only through governed processes and SHALL NOT destructively overwrite history.

**Conformance criteria:** Predecessor, successor, reason, effective boundary and affected scope remain explicit.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-037 — Partial Semantic Supersession

**Applicability:** Every supersession affecting only part of a governed meaning. **Normative force:** SHALL. **Traceability:** Semantic evolution. **Expected evidence:** Affected and unaffected component manifests.

Partial supersession SHALL identify exact semantic components, relationships, scopes or applicability regions replaced.

**Conformance criteria:** Unaffected meaning remains valid and independently distinguishable.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-038 — Historical Semantic Reproducibility

**Applicability:** Every historical engineering artefact and interpretation. **Normative force:** SHALL / SHALL NOT. **Traceability:** TS-STD-001; TS-STD-004. **Expected evidence:** Historical replay against exact dependencies.

Historical meaning SHALL remain reproducible against the exact Ontology, Vocabulary, entity, Assertion, relationship, mapping and consumer revisions originally applicable and SHALL NOT be silently reinterpreted using current semantics.

**Conformance criteria:** Replay reproduces prior meaning without promoting it into current Engineering Truth.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-039 — Consumer Semantic Fidelity

**Applicability:** Every semantic consumer. **Normative force:** SHALL / SHALL NOT. **Traceability:** Cross-domain boundaries. **Expected evidence:** Knowledge, Correlation, Explanation, Decision and Presentation audits.

Consumers SHALL preserve meaning, identity, revision, context, applicability, qualification, ambiguity, conflict and lifecycle and SHALL NOT create, strengthen, generalise or reconstruct missing meaning.

**Conformance criteria:** Consumer output remains traceable to exact governed semantic material.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-040 — Presentation Non-Reinterpretation

**Applicability:** Every Presentation representation. **Normative force:** SHALL / SHALL NOT. **Traceability:** Presentation boundary. **Expected evidence:** UI, report and summary audits.

Presentation MAY simplify wording only where semantic fidelity remains intact and SHALL NOT redefine meaning, conceal qualification or convert ambiguity into certainty.

**Conformance criteria:** Material meaning remains recoverable and unchanged at every Presentation depth.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-041 — Artificial Intelligence Semantic Non-Authority

**Applicability:** AI-generated or AI-assisted terminology, mapping, translation and interpretation. **Normative force:** SHALL NOT. **Traceability:** TS-STD-001; TS-STD-002; TS-STD-003. **Expected evidence:** AI provenance and governance audits.

Artificial intelligence output SHALL NOT independently establish canonical meaning, semantic equivalence, Ontology classification, governed terminology or relationship kind.

**Conformance criteria:** AI-originated material remains identified, qualified and governed before canonical use.

### TS-STD-005-REQ-042 — Domain-Owned Semantic Content

**Applicability:** Every Engineering Domain defining or consuming domain-specific meaning. **Normative force:** SHALL. **Traceability:** Engineering Domain ownership. **Expected evidence:** Domain semantic policies and ownership records.

Actual engineering meanings SHALL remain owned by the responsible Architecture or Engineering Domain while conforming to this Standard's integrity obligations.

**Conformance criteria:** TS-STD-005 governs preservation without becoming the source of domain meaning.

## 13. Consumer Obligations

Consumers preserve exact semantic identity and revision, represented subject, Ontology classification, Vocabulary expression, semantic context, applicability, conditions, exclusions, limitations, relationship kind and direction, ambiguity, conflict, qualification, provenance, lineage, lifecycle and historical interpretation where applicable.

Consumers shall not infer missing meaning from labels, filenames, storage paths, schema names, display order, repetition, statistical similarity, source reputation, successful serialization, availability or convention. A consumer unable to preserve required meaning shall fail visibly or return a qualified unresolved result.

## 14. Cross-Standard Boundaries

TS-STD-001 governs Engineering Truth. TS-STD-002 governs Evidence integrity. TS-STD-003 governs Qualification, Authority, Verification and Confidence. TS-STD-004 governs universal Identity, Revision, Representation, Reference, Provenance, Lineage and Traceability. TS-STD-006 will govern Knowledge admission and publication integrity.

TS-STD-005 preserves meaning across those concerns without assuming their ownership.

## 15. Non-Ownership

TS-STD-005 does not define Engineering Truth, Engineering Evidence, Qualification, Authority, Verification, Confidence, universal Identity architecture, Ontology contents, Vocabulary contents, Knowledge Entity contents, Engineering Assertion content, manufacturer mappings, domain classifications, admission, publication, Registry implementation, Explanation, Decision, Presentation, UI language, APIs, schemas, persistence, algorithms or runtime technology.

## 16. Conformance

Potential subjects include Ontology and Vocabulary architectures, Knowledge and Assertion contracts, semantic relationships, terminology mappings, translation processes, serialization, persistence and API contracts, Registry records, historical replay, Knowledge consumers and AI-assisted semantic processes.

Assessments identify exact subject and version, this Standard version, applicable requirement revisions, method, evidence, assessment Authority, findings, exceptions, outcome and lifecycle.

Passing tests may demonstrate preservation of declared semantics. They do not prove that underlying meaning faithfully represents engineering reality.

## 17. Expected Conformance Evidence

Expected evidence may include semantic identity and revision manifests, Ontology and Vocabulary references, concept definitions, proposition and relationship specifications, synonymy, homonymy and ambiguity records, manufacturer mappings, compatibility assessments, translation and transformation specifications, loss manifests, round trips, consumer audits, historical replay, correction and supersession history, negative equivalence and causation tests, Presentation reviews and AI-origin records.

## 18. Compatibility and Evolution

The requirements are foundational. Material change to the Semantic model, semantic identity boundaries, equivalence classifications, ambiguity or conflict meaning, relationship semantics, manufacturer mapping, change boundaries, historical interpretation, consumer obligations or AI boundaries requires governed compatibility review.

Migration preserves exact historical meaning and shall not silently apply current Ontology, Vocabulary or semantic mappings to historical material.

## 19. Permanent Prohibitions

Prohibited conduct includes deriving canonical meaning from labels; treating identical labels as identity or equivalence; treating different labels as proof of different meaning; alias laundering; synonymy treated as identity; similarity treated as equivalence; association or correlation treated as causation; classification treated as Qualification; terminology treated as Authority; ungoverned source-native promotion; translation assumed equivalent; silent scope inflation; concealed ambiguity or conflict; consumer-, Presentation-, persistence-, schema- or API-created meaning; retrospective current semantics; silent relationship-kind substitution; silent semantic change beneath unchanged revision; manufacturer-specific meaning promoted to universal; destructive historical rewriting; AI terminology promoted through plausibility or repetition; removal of material context; serialization success treated as compatibility; and terminology or implementation preserved at the expense of engineering reality.
